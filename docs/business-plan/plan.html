Sidenote: Business & Build Plan

Nov 13, 2026·@vineet

Overview

Sidenote is a website where you upload a PDF or your notes and ask questions in plain language. The AI answers only from your pages, and every answer links to the exact line it came from, so you can check it in one click. There is nothing to install, and visitors can try it before signing up.

  • Product: a document Q&A website with numbered citations and a highlighted source page
  • For: students first, then researchers and working professionals
  • Revenue model: free plan (limited questions) + paid plans from ₹149/month (estimate)
  • Why it can work: our bet is that people do not trust a chatbot answer without proof, and that few tools show the exact source line while also handling Hindi and Hinglish well. We still have to confirm this with real users.

All numbers in this document are estimates. After the first 20-30 users, replace them with real measurements.

Problem and target customers

People already paste documents into chatbots, but they cannot tell whether the answer is real or invented. Finding the right page by hand in a 200-page PDF takes too long. Sidenote gives the answer and the page together.

Customer typeTheir problemWhat they need
Students (college, exam prep)Long textbooks and notes; hard to find where something is written; cannot trust an answer without the pageAsk a question, get the answer and the exact page, in Hindi or English
Researchers and project studentsMany papers; need specific facts and where each one appearedQuestions across several files, with citations
Working professionalsLong contracts, reports and manuals; little time to readQuick answers with proof of where they came from

Who to sell to first: students. They are easy to reach (college groups, Instagram, study communities), they feel the pain before every exam, and they accept ₹99-149/month if it clearly saves time. Professionals and businesses come later and can pay more.

Market size: I do not have a reliable number. Talk to 50 students first and see how many would pay; only then estimate the market.

User flow

The user only needs a browser. The full flow:

  1. The visitor opens the site and drops in a PDF or notes. No sign-up is needed for the first try.
  2. Sidenote reads the file and splits it into passages. This takes a few seconds.
  3. The user asks a question in English, Hindi or Hinglish (for example "photosynthesis kahan hoti hai?").
  4. The answer streams in with numbered citations, such as [1] and [2].
  5. The user clicks a citation. A source page opens with the matching sentence highlighted.
  6. When the free limit is reached, the user signs in with Google and picks a plan. Payment goes through Razorpay.
  7. Later visits show the saved library and past chats, so the user can continue where they stopped.

If the answer is not in the documents, Sidenote says so instead of guessing. A clear "not found" is better than a confident wrong answer.

Tech stack and architecture

architecture · 5 components

The browser sends files and questions to the Sidenote server. The server saves the text as searchable passages, finds the best ones for each question, and sends only those passages to the AI model. The model's answer streams back with citations. Razorpay tells the server when a payment succeeds, so plans change automatically.

Tech choices

  • Backend: Python + FastAPI (already built and working)
  • Front end: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript (already built); move to a framework only when it gets hard to maintain
  • AI model: start with a hosted model API, a small and cheap model first, from a provider that does not train on API data. Move to your own GPU (vLLM + a 7B model) only when volume justifies it (see costs). The code already supports both.
  • Search: keyword search now; multilingual embeddings (for example bge-m3) with pgvector in days 31-60
  • Database: PostgreSQL. Supabase is an option if you want database, login and storage in one place and less setup time.
  • Deploy: Docker, one small VPS, Caddy for HTTPS, GitHub Actions for tests and deploys
  • Payment: Razorpay subscriptions (Stripe later for customers outside India)

Break-even, worked example. Assume fixed cost of ₹3,000/month and a hosted model at ₹0.5 per question. A Plus user asks about 100 questions a month, so AI costs ₹50, and the payment fee is about ₹3. That leaves about ₹96 per user, and ₹3,000 / ₹96 means roughly 32 paying users cover the fixed cost. At 500 paying Plus users: revenue ₹74,500, variable cost ₹26,500, fixed ₹3,000, so about ₹45,000/month before tax. If your measured cost per question comes out above about ₹1, change the model or tighten the limits before you start charging.

Free users cost money too. At 20 questions and ₹0.5 each, a fully active free user costs at most ₹10 a month. 1,000 active free users could cost up to ₹10,000, so keep the free limit small.

Privacy, legal and trust

Documents can be private: notes, contracts, reports. People only stay if they trust you, so set these rules from day one:

  • Consent: say clearly before upload what is stored, for how long and why, with a link to the privacy policy.
  • Keep as little as possible: today files live only in memory and are deleted after 2 hours. When you add saved libraries, let users choose how long to keep files.
  • Delete option: a "delete my account and all files" button that removes the database rows, the stored files and the search data.
  • Never sell data: do not share or sell user documents; say so in the policy.
  • AI provider policy: confirm in writing that the API does not train on your data and check its retention rules.
  • No document text in logs or analytics events.
  • Honest disclaimer: "Answers are written by an AI and can be wrong. Check the sources. This is not legal, medical or financial advice."
  • Sensitive files: warn users not to upload IDs, bank or medical records unless you have the controls to protect them.

In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies to personal data. Before you start charging, have a lawyer check the privacy policy and the consent wording, and ask a chartered accountant about GST and invoicing.

90-day build and launch plan

90-day roadmap · 3 phases, 2 gates

The plan assumes 8-10 hours a week: 1 hour on each weeknight, 3 hours on Saturday and 2 on Sunday. That is about 40 hours per phase. In exam weeks, keep only the Sunday slot. If a gate is not passed, spend 2 more weeks in the same phase; do not add new features.

Phase 1: days 1-30, beta online (about 40 hours)

  • Put the code in git, add tests for upload and search, and set up CI (8 h)
  • Deploy to a VPS with HTTPS, connect a hosted AI model, add error tracking and analytics (12 h)
  • Add thumbs up/down, a feedback form and a short privacy and terms page (6 h)
  • Invite 20-30 users, talk to 10 of them (10 h)
  • Measure the real cost per question with 20 real PDFs (4 h)

Gate 1: 20 people used it, 10 of them asked at least 3 questions, you know your cost per question, and at least 5 people say they would pay.

Phase 2: days 31-60, make it stick (about 40 hours)

  • Google sign-in and a PostgreSQL database (16 h)
  • Saved library and chat history (12 h)
  • Multilingual embedding search so Hindi and Hinglish questions find English text (8 h)
  • A test set of 40 questions to measure answer quality (4 h)

Gate 2: 30 signed-in users and 10 of them still use it a week later. The "not found" answer works on the unanswerable test questions.

Phase 3: days 61-90, charge and launch (about 40 hours)

  • Razorpay subscription and usage limits (16 h)
  • Pricing page and reviewed legal pages (6 h)
  • Demo document and first-run onboarding (4 h)
  • Launch posts and short demo videos (10 h)
  • Buffer for fixes (4 h)

Day 90 check: 100 free users, 3 real paying users, and the margin per paying user is positive.

The PDF page viewer, notebook, study tools and more file types come after day 90. The detailed engineering steps are in DEVELOPMENT_PLAN.md.

Marketing and first 100 users

The first 100 users will come from talking to people directly, not from paid ads. Start ads only after 20-30 users clearly like the product.

  1. Weeks 1-2: give the site to 10-15 friends and classmates and ask for feedback every few days.
  2. Weeks 3-4: share it once in college and exam-prep groups, with a short demo video (not spam).
  3. Month 2: short videos on Instagram, YouTube Shorts and LinkedIn: "Ask your textbook a question and see the exact page", using real screen recordings.
  4. Referral: when a friend joins, both get 10 extra questions.
  5. Build in public: one LinkedIn post a week: what you built, how many users, what you learned.
  6. Later: landing pages for use cases (study notes, research papers, contracts) so search engines can find you.

Target (estimate): 100 free users, about 5-10% of them paying. If conversion is far lower, change the pricing or the features, not the marketing.
